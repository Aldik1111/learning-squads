# Talk about tomorrow in French

A simple plan needs an activity and enough time information for the listener to understand it. We will describe a future activity with `aller` plus an infinitive, then ask and confirm the details.

## Reuse a short pattern

`Je vais lire` means "I am going to read." Change only the infinitive to build another plan: `Je vais étudier` (I am going to study) or `Je vais marcher` (I am going to walk). Do not insert `à` between `vais` and the infinitive in this future pattern.

Add `aujourd’hui` for today or `demain` for tomorrow. To ask politely about your partner's plan, use `Qu’est-ce que vous allez faire demain ?` (What are you going to do tomorrow?). `À quelle heure ?` asks "At what time?" and `À dix heures` means "At ten o'clock."

## Try an exchange

A: `Demain, je vais étudier.`

B: `À quelle heure ?`

A: `À dix heures du matin.`

B: `D’accord, demain à dix heures du matin.`

The last line repeats the agreement. It is useful to clarify morning or evening; for real online arrangements, also confirm the actual date and time zone in a language both people understand. This practice dialogue does not itself reserve an appointment.

## Practice

Prepare two plans using different infinitives. Ask your partner about tomorrow, ask for the time, and repeat what you understood. Then role-play a change from ten to eleven: `À onze heures`. Write a fictional dialogue showing the updated plan. In the task summary, explain why you kept the second verb in its infinitive form and how the final line removes ambiguity.

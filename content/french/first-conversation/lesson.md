# Start a friendly conversation in French

Your first conversation can be short and useful. Learn a few reusable phrases, listen for the response, and ask for help when needed. You may use a fictional first name; you do not need to disclose private information to practice.

## Build a polite opening

- `Bonjour.` — Hello / good day.
- `Je m’appelle Alex.` — My name is Alex.
- `Et vous ?` — And you? (polite address)
- `Je parle un peu français.` — I speak a little French.
- `Pouvez-vous répéter, s’il vous plaît ?` — Could you repeat, please?
- `Merci. Au revoir.` — Thank you. Goodbye.

This lesson starts with `vous` as a polite way to address one new partner. French also uses `vous` for more than one person. `Tu` is familiar singular address; its use depends on the relationship and context. Agree with your partner before switching, rather than mixing forms at random.

## Try an exchange

A: `Bonjour. Je m’appelle Alex. Et vous ?`

B: `Bonjour. Je m’appelle Camille.`

A: `Pouvez-vous répéter, s’il vous plaît ?`

B: `Camille.`

A: `Merci. Je parle un peu français.`

Asking for repetition is part of conversation, not a failure. Listen to a partner or the linked learning resources for pronunciation; English spelling approximations can hide important sounds. Focus first on being understood, then choose one phrase to improve.

## Practice

Write a six-line greeting with a fictional name, a return question, a repetition request, and a goodbye. Say it aloud, then try without reading every word. In your task summary, describe one phrase you improved. Materials and the quiz are available even when no conversation partner is online; the conversation guide gives you a separate practice agenda.

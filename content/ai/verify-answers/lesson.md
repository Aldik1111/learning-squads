# Check a confident answer against evidence

A fluent answer can still contain an unsupported detail. Treat a generated answer as a draft to inspect. Confidence, polished formatting, and a citation-shaped link are not themselves evidence that the claim is correct.

## Use a small evidence table

Our fictional source note says: "The workshop begins Tuesday at 10:00. Participants bring a laptop. The room will be announced later." A draft says: "Join Tuesday at 10:00 in Room 4; a laptop is provided."

Break the draft into claims. The time is supported. Room 4 is unsupported because the room is still unknown. The laptop statement conflicts with the note. Write a corrected version: "The workshop begins Tuesday at 10:00. Bring a laptop. The room has not been announced."

For each claim, record the exact supporting passage or mark it unsupported. Distinguish "not found" from "false": a source may simply omit a fact. If the answer cites an external page, open the actual page and check that it supports this specific claim, is relevant to the requested date, and is an appropriate authority. Do not ask the same model to certify its own answer and treat that as independent verification.

## Practice

Use this fictional note: "The study room opens at 09:00 on Friday. Entry is free. Booking is required. Capacity is not stated." Inspect: "Drop in Friday at 09:00, pay $5, and join up to 30 people."

Create a claim/evidence/verdict table and a corrected announcement. Keep the unsupported capacity unknown. You can do this exercise without an AI account: the supplied draft is enough. If you use a tool, share only fictional data and do the source comparison yourself. Your task result should show the reasoning, not merely claim that the answer was checked.

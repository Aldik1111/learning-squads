# Ask clearly and check the result

You can practise this lesson without an AI account. Use the fictional notes and draft below; no external AI service is required.

## Make the request testable

Before asking for a draft, write four things: the task, the facts it may use, the audience, and the output format. Add what to do when information is missing.

Fictional notes:

- The team check-in is on Tuesday.
- Agenda: progress, blockers, next steps.
- The organizer has not supplied a time or location.

Example request:

> Using only these notes, write a friendly reminder for teammates in at most 80 words. Include the three agenda items. If a time or location is missing, say it is still to be confirmed.

## Inspect a flawed draft

This is a deliberately authored example, not output from a connected model:

> Join us Tuesday at 10:00 in Room 4 to discuss progress, blockers and next steps. Attendance is required.

Which parts are supported? Tuesday and the agenda come from the notes. The time, room and attendance requirement do not. Remove those additions or ask the organizer to confirm them.

A corrected example:

> Our team check-in is on Tuesday. We will discuss progress, blockers and next steps. The time and location are still to be confirmed.

## Practise independently

Write your own request for a three-bullet version of this reminder. Then check a draft against each supplied fact and each format requirement. Ask a teammate which unsupported detail would be easiest to miss.

Treat generated text as a draft to review. Fluent wording does not establish truth. When an answer makes a factual claim, check the relevant original source; an included link alone is not verification. Use invented or redacted examples for practice instead of private customer records or credentials.

# Make a simple plan in Spanish

A useful conversation ends with both people understanding the same plan. You do not need a long speech: state an activity, ask about availability, and repeat the agreed details.

## A small set of reusable phrases

- `Hoy voy a estudiar.` — Today I am going to study.
- `Mañana voy a leer.` — Tomorrow I am going to read.
- `¿Qué vas a hacer mañana?` — What are you going to do tomorrow?
- `¿A qué hora?` — At what time?
- `A las seis.` — At six o'clock.
- `¿Puedes hablar mañana?` — Can you talk tomorrow?

For your own plan, use `voy a` followed by an infinitive such as `leer`, `estudiar`, or `caminar`. Keep the small word `a`. When asking a familiar partner, `vas a` is the corresponding form with `tú`. This lesson uses informal singular address; agree with your partner if another form feels more appropriate.

## Try an exchange

A: `Mañana voy a estudiar. ¿Puedes hablar a las seis?`

B: `Sí, a las seis de la tarde.`

A: `Entonces, mañana a las seis de la tarde.`

The final line confirms rather than adds a new arrangement. Online partners may live in different time zones: agree on the actual date and zone separately instead of assuming that six means the same instant for both people.

## Practice

Write two plans, one for today and one for tomorrow. Ask your partner about one plan, then change a proposed time and confirm the replacement. Use `¿Puedes repetir, por favor?` if you need repetition. In your task result, write a fictional six-line dialogue and explain which line confirms the day and time. Do not publish a real appointment or a partner's personal information.

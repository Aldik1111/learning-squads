# Find an activity you both enjoy

Finding something in common gives a beginner conversation a clear purpose. You can like different things and still understand each other. The aim is to ask, listen, and suggest an activity both people accept.

## Likes and choices

`Me gusta leer` means "I like reading." With a single thing, say `Me gusta el café` (I like coffee). With a plural noun, use `Me gustan los libros` (I like books). For the patterns in this lesson, the activity or thing liked determines `gusta` versus `gustan`; do not choose `gustan` just because several people are talking.

To ask a familiar partner, use `¿Te gusta caminar?` (Do you like walking?). To offer a choice, ask `¿Prefieres leer o caminar?` (Do you prefer reading or walking?). `Prefiero caminar` gives a preference rather than a claim that the other option is bad.

## Try an exchange

A: `Me gusta leer. ¿Y a ti?` — I like reading. And you?

B: `A mí también, pero hoy prefiero caminar.` — Me too, but today I prefer walking.

A: `De acuerdo. Podemos caminar.` — Agreed. We can walk.

`No me gusta...` is a straightforward way to express a dislike. Use an ordinary, respectful tone; you do not need to invent agreement to keep a conversation friendly.

## Practice

Prepare one sentence about an activity, one about a singular thing, and one about plural things. Ask a partner two questions and propose a fictional activity. Change the proposal if your preferences differ. In your task summary, show a short dialogue and explain one choice between `gusta` and `gustan`. Keep the example fictional and avoid sharing another person's preferences as if they had agreed to publication.
